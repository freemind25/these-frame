Introduction
A
cademic writing is the term used for scientific papers written to show an objective stance, organized in such a way that they can easily be replicated by others. And, although many assume that an academic paper must take the form of a thesis, this isn’t always true. Academic documents include a wider broad of papers, such as the Book Report, Conference Paper, Dissertation, Essay, Research Article, Research Paper and Technical Report.
Another element to academic writing that few know about is that, in any give topic and under any methodology, the author or authors, must assume a stating point, a perspective exposed under the form of a hypothesis. Ultimately, any paper starts with a subjective approach, an assumption, that is then tested and verified, proved or disproved. Furthermore, a research paper is always based on documented data reviewed about a certain topic, in order to justify itself as a new step towards a better understanding, and not merely a repetition of previous discoveries. This new step, however, doesn’t need to be intended forward, and can merely refer to a new perspective about something previously studied or known.
For the purpose of presenting a qualifiable research with adequate and sophisticated results, students and researches must conduct an investigation under certain rules and formalized within a specific methodology. It is this methodology that will then allow the necessary acquisition of skills through trial and error, while providing a fruitful ground to learn from mistakes shown along the way, and then correct any necessary procedure being conducted.
Taking into account the experience of the author as a college lecturer on the filed of academic writing and research in China, but also as a researcher and consultant for many companies from different countries, namely, in Europe, China and the USA, always predicting the outcome verified, this book was prepared to help others in similar fields of work. The intention here is to allow an easy and practical attitude to investigation, one that anyone can easily understand, follow and apply on his own.
More than helping college students in creating a better thesis, this book can help anyone in the field of investigation, in knowing which steps to follow to create strong, valid and interesting arguments on any subject, and that can then be applied to reach valuable outcomes, on a personal or social level. Here, you’ll find guidelines related to how one can think and research more effectively.

Differences Between Academic Writing and Simple Descriptions
T
here are significant differences between what one thinks and what one knows as a fact, and they can be easily identified in how we describe something or a certain procedure. In the first place, an academic paper must start with a specific question, an hypothesis, which, from the paper standpoint, must be then proved or disproved, and finally concluded as regarding a why. From a different viewpoint, a description won’t have a specific goal, or when having one, won’t show more that assumptions and personal insights, but nothing that can easily be verifiable by others. It’s actually interesting to notice that, most people, when trying to win an argument, will add more insights and opinions to it, to make the main point defused and difficult to justify, rather than showing proves against the main hypothesis. And that’s how we know that someone lacks reasons to be credible. And, nevertheless, such experiences also show us why it’s so difficult to find good researchers. A research requires a certain level of mental capability, sanity and detachment from emotional perceptions, that, quite frankly, very few people in the world have. One must be able to detach from his own ego and need to win an argument in order to truly see truth for what it is, especially if that truth demands a complete reconsideration for one’s personal values and beliefs.
It’s as correct to say that writing an academic paper requires effort, as it is to say that those efforts are at the level of the mental skills one must develop to communicate efficiently. Writing an academic paper isn’t an art, but a mental ability that must be exercised with persistence and discernment. That is the source of good arguments. Any creativity and originality that an experiment may seem to demand, ends with the conscious effort to put such attitude into perspective and an analysis that can make the results predictable by future researchers. In fact, a strong paper will be backed by strong arguments, i.e., conclusions made by other equally valuable researches within the same field. This is how one’s paper value is increased in its social and public validity.

Stages in the Writing of an Academic Paper
R
esearch papers are often structured under the IMRAD format:
•
Introduction:
Is a complete set of knowledge on the topic justifying further research and the formation of a suitable strategy for the investigation. This preliminary investigation on what is known may often not justify a certain research or, instead, redirect it towards a less known answer or answered question;
•
Methodology:
Is composed of one or more methods of analysis, in order to obtain data that can be correlated, compared, measured and analyzed, either deductively or inductively;
•
Results:
The information obtained from the research conducted;
•
Analysis:
Consist in a crossing of the data obtained in order to see patterns and paradigms emerging from the investigation that may allow strong arguments in favor or against the main hypothesis;
•
Discussion:
It implies a correlation between the analysis and the purpose of the investigation, often indicating in which direction future investigations should go in order to obtain more information within the study in process.
This said, the introduction should start with the question “Why?”, why should I do this this research? And the method is related to how I will do it, while the results will show me what I need to see about it, and the discussion is the “so What?”, or in other words, the leading arguments towards the meaning of the information obtained. Any research must be driven by a practical purpose, which must somehow be applicable by others, and socially necessary as well, even though most researches are made for governmental purposes. Any research proves itself interesting and necessary if driven by the altruistic goal of fulfilling a certain need, and not merely for being entertaining or justifiable by monetary gains.
Common to all research papers is an elaboration of questions such as the following:
•
What do I know about my topic?
•
Can I answer the questions who, what, when, where, why, how?
•
What do I know about the context of my topic?
•
What historical or cultural influences do I know about that might be important to my topic?
•
Does my topic belong to any particular genre or category of topics?
•
What do I know about this genre?
•
What seems important to me about this topic?
•
If I were to summarize what I know about this topic, what points would I focus  on?
•
What points seem less important? Why do I think so?
•
How does this topic relate to other things that I know?
•
What do I know about the topic that might help my reader to understand it in new ways?
Sometimes it is also useful to reflect on the opposite perspective when thinking about how to address a particular topic:
•
What don’t I know about my topic?
•
What do I need to know?
•
How can I find out more?
•
What do I think, and why do I think that?

The Preparation of a Research
W
hen preparing a research one must choose an appropriate topic. But how? Students often find this to be the most difficult task, because they start from a viewpoint that is impossible to know everything that is known or not known in a certain topic. And that isn’t necessarily true. You can start by asking yourself the following questions:
•
What is the topic of the analysis?
•
What can I find related to it?
•
In which direction are similar researches pointing?
•
What kind of questions were not asked before?
•
Do you have a personal curiosity regarding the information not yet found?
•
For whom can your research be useful if you move forward with it?
These questions can be resumed in 3 main procedures:
Prepare: Analyze your question, rephrase it, rethink it and formulate better questions;
Research: Adopt a position and start creating several hypothesis that can lead your research strategy and help you formulate good methods;
Write: At this point, writing anything includes drafts, related to your position, perspective and expectations, as well as possible conclusions, in favor or against your initial set of hypothesis.
In order to understand how these principles can be applied, consider interesting researches already conducted by others. For example, BBC has conducted, among others, several investigations on gender difference, attraction and relationships. Pick one that interests you the most and try to answer the following questions:
What is the title of the research?
What is the purpose of the research?
What was the common procedure (similarities) for each method applied?
Which method seems more accurate/effective and why?
What can we conclude from the research?
How would you repeat the same research?
Which method can be useful for you in the future?
Don’t worry if your answers were wrong. The purpose of this exercise is to help you organize your own methods of work. Therefore, once you finish observing everything, review your answers, and compare them to understand how a research is conducted. Then, do the same with other researches until you are clear about your methods of work.

Basic Research Assignments
M
ost people assume that doing a research is something very complex. However, the truth is that there are many levels to a research. The following are simple assignments that you can perform without any guidance to practice your ability to observe and analyze information.
Assignment Nr.1: Am I a Friendly Person?
As an experiment and motivational guidance to help you understand how to do a research easily, consider the following question about yourself: Am I a Friendly Person?
Using this simple question, follow a practical online method of research, such as Facebook Chat, QQ, email, phone call, or other that seems better to you, and ask your friends this simple question. Basically, you’ll ask, at least, 3 of your friends what they think about you, and if they think that you are a friendly person or not. Analyze then their answers by looking at similarities, repetitive concepts and arguments. If you can’t find them, proceed with more people, until such pattern emerges.
As you will eventually discover, there is one clear reason why they think you are, or not, a good friend, and that reason is what makes you valuable or not to them. Maybe the reason will surprise you or not, but the main point here is that you are allowing them to show you what is that they see in you that they rarely see in others. This insight will make you see reality from another angle, and realize that indeed human beings quantify their emotions and measure their friendships through specific values, either they’re conscious of it or not.
Assignment Nr.2: Comparing Different Thesis
When finishing the previous exercise, move to the next. Search for academic papers written by different authors and about the same topic. Ideally, you would choose 3 papers. You may use the following websites for your search. Choose one from the following list of websites:
http://www.literature-study-online.com/essays/
http://www.princeton.edu/main/news/archive/S35/82/65G58/
http://www.ssrn.com/
When your choice is made, describe what they have in common and their main differences.
Assignment Nr.3: Are you a Special Person?
Do you think that you are a special person? Answer this question by following the method described below:
Choose a small number of friends or roommates;
Describe who they are and for how long they know you;
Describe their most common opinions on you;
Write your conclusions regarding the opinions you’ve heard from them;
Finally, define your best quality, what you should change in your personality, and answer the main question of the essay, taking into account the results of your research.

Choose a paper obtained from http://www.ssrn.com/
Now, answer the following questions:

The Big Picture
O
nce you have a perspective on how an academic paper is produced, is time to look at the big picture. In order to do that, you start with a general purpose, related to the subject you wish to understand, and then move to a specific purpose, varying according to the assignment. Once this is made, you must demonstrate the capability of breaking down ideas, to promote a better understanding, and this skill demands 3 competences:
•
Deductive Reasoning: Stating the main idea of a thesis and then following with supporting examples;
•
Semiformal voice: This means avoiding common expressions of ordinary speech, contractions of nouns and verbs, etc;
•
Third person point-of-view: Third person points-of-view are “he”, “she”, “it”, and “they” as well as their accusative, dative, and possessive forms.
When describing your intention behind the research, you must use arguments and persuasion, justifying in this way why the reader should believe that your research is necessary. Some of the most important questions that need to be answered are the following:
•
What is it?
•
What does it do?
•
What does it resemble?
•
How does it work?
•
How does it come about?
•
Why is it important?
You then formulate your hypothesis and questions that will lead to the creation of certain methodologies, by explaining how you will analyze cause and effect, how you’ll compare and contrast results and how the process will, one way or another, lead you to such outcome. In fact, a well controlled methodology is always one that, independently on the effects, can explain one cause or another.
There must also always be a certain coherence in your writing, which facilitates the reading and understanding:
•
Connect the paragraphs by effective links related to one another;
•
Support your ideas with quotations from reliable sources,
•
Use transitional expressions, such as “therefore”, “however”, “for example”, “finally”, etc.
Whenever possible, support your arguments furthermore, by adding examples taken from other researches and mentioning them by author and year, such as: “Academic Writing” (N. Mars, 2016).
You may also use paraphrasing. In this case, you’re taking another person’s arguments and rewriting them on your own words. These arguments are presented as if you were quoting, except that in this case there are no quotation marks, since you’ve change the structure of the original sentence.
Finally, you must summarize everything  by presenting the main thoughts and ideas. Make sure that your reader...
•
Achieves deeper knowledge on the subject by reading your paper;
•
Can check relevant paraphrases and quotations to confirm your statements;
•
Can replicate your experiment based on what is written.
Don’t forget that, in academic writing, you must be responsible for everything you say or write, and, as such, you must be capable of providing evidence or justifications about absolutely everything that you produce, write or say. That’s also exactly the same way that you use to protect yourself against the attacks of those that may try to discredit your work.

Research Methodologies
A
methodology will always depend on many variables but typically follows one of 3 types:
•
Quantitative:
Approaches any result through a quantifiable evidence, and often statistical analysis as well. It’s the most common approach in biology, chemistry, medicine, consultancy, finance, business, etc. Here, results become predictable based on the information obtained and measured, patterns that keep emerging. Ex: Cluster Analysis, Correlation and Association, Multivariate Statistics, Regression Analysis, Surveys and Questionnaire, Structural Equation Modeling, Survey Research, Quantitative Marketing Research;
•
Qualitative:
This type of methodology is the most common to study social phenomena, and operates through direct observation, communication and subjective discernment. It’s used by psychologists, psychiatrists, sociologists, pedagogues, and so on. Ex: Analytic Induction, Case Study, Ethnography, Life History, Morphological Analysis, Participant Observation, Textual Analysis, Unstructured Interview;
•
Mixed or Crossed Methodology:
In some cases, a mixed methodology is applied, namely, when we need to measure patterns emerging from social interactions. This is the case when a research is conducted for governmental purposes, to predict elections, to analyze thinking patterns in groups of people, or to conduct social experiments on a larger scale, either for administrative or security reasons, namely, when we want to measure the impact of the mainstream media on people. A famous example is also the use of keywords on internet. Google Trends, for example, uses a combination of both qualitative and quantitative methodologies, and has proven to be very useful for entrepreneurs and business owners to study trends in the market. Ex: Archival Research, Content Analysis, Longitudinal Study, Focus Group, Historical Method, Semi-Structured Interview, Structured Interview.
You should also remember that a good research must always allow for the existence of a surprise on the researcher, i.e., the methodology shouldn’t be controlled to a level in which the results are predictable from the start and can easily be manipulated by the observer’s belief system. On the contrary, the results must guide the researcher through variables that should reveal a pattern on their own, and as much independently as possible.
Finally, the researcher should never conduct any type of research that is deprived of morality, ethics, social justice or, in any way, disrespect other human beings. Any methodology that, for example, intends to compare people as black, white, yellow and brown skin color is racist from the start, and can only reinforce social discrimination, independently of the results, because assuming that people can be different based on their skin color is a prejudice that can’t justify itself for any other reason than racist segregation of individuals in a society.
The same applies to studies that intend to prove the stratification of social classes or the level of intelligence of the individuals based on their nationality. As a matter of fact, Nazi Germany, the Soviet Union, or the China under the control of Mao Zedong, contain great historical examples of researches that should have never been conducted, for the simple fact that they satisfy anti-humanitarian and totalitarian agendas.
In more modern times we have psychiatry conducting illegal experiments on patients, in which the results can seriously damage the mind of the patient for all his life.
No research is perfect by itself, but a good researcher is always attentive to what was just now described and intends to minimize risks while maximizing the potential importance of his work for others, and not just specific individuals within a society.

The Importance of Good Analytical Skills
T
he fundamental principles applied to conduct a research can lead one to unlimited insights on life, personality, intelligence, chemistry, energy and any other topics related to existence. This lack of limitation comes from the fact that nothing is 100% certain, and as such, we can only work with  probabilities. As a matter of fact, this method is the same applied by good fortunetellers, and, if the scientific community knew this, Academic Research wouldn’t seem so complex or lead to arrogant assumptions. Because, you see, both the scientist and the fortuneteller are trying to predict future events, by merely applying different methods, which in some cases seem more credible than others, even though they can and should be perfectly compatible. Quantum Mechanics is now helping us merge these two kingdoms of energy and matter.
Despite the fact that science is stratified in different fields of study, there’s always a combination of different elements interacting between each other in order to produce a certain result, and we may not ever be able to control them all. For example, a child with learning disabilities may simply have a crazy mother that will never accept any kind of correction on her behavior.
Ultimately, the success of a good research, as in one’s life, is based on...
•
The Control of a Process -
The more we can predict an outcome, the more we can guarantee the quality of a certain technique. For example, business owners, car racing drivers and football players, always follow the same principles. The success of famous individuals on such unpredictable fields depends on their understanding of certain strategies related to human behavior, which they apply on their competitors, and in obtaining a certain goal, i.e., winning a race against other drivers, for example;
•
The Development of Skills -
A technique is not enough without a good skill, and that’s why many so called Martial Arts Masters get heavily beaten when trying to confront a Street Fighter in a Mixed Martial Arts Match, as these individuals have a higher Skill with less techniques. As Bruce Lee used to say, “I fear not the man who has practiced ten thousand kicks once, but I fear the man who has practiced one kick ten thousand times”. And yet, in studying a topic too, is more important to practice several times a technique that works to learn faster, than to always study different topics in different ways. The most effective students with higher results in their exams aren’t worried about the topic or the questions of an exam, but only on how much they can learn on a certain topic;
•
The Study of Similarities and Differences
- Just like in Academic Writing, we must always follow the principles of how to do the Analysis of Information, to extract a specific principle and following-up technique after that. That is, if you know, for example, how students think, it’s easier to plan an efficient teaching method. For example, the Educational System is extremely efficient, because it can force unhappy and unmotivated students to sit quietly in a classroom, paying attention to someone that they didn’t choose to listen, among a group of people they didn’t choose to be with, every day, for 6 to 8 hours a day, and it’s all based on the studying of how to control a nation and the mind on individuals, i.e., Pavlovian Studies on dogs and children. The IQ Test is also efficient because it’s not about Intelligence, but merely social selection, therefore matching the educational system and the exam scores of the students. But, once you know how to measure Similarities and Differences, you can then differentiate information more clearly within each group of analysis and even predict results in advance with a certain high amount of probabilities. Examples of such cases apply when you’re trying to compare Good and Bad students, Good and Bad Musicians, Good and Bad Business Owner, etc.
Once you understand that everything in life depends on techniques and strategies, and not luck, gifs or randomly, you will be able to more easily accept the fact that conscience can and often directs events towards the most unpredictable cases. That’s how you have people in the world that can cut a bullet at 200 km/h using a Samurai sword, or how amateur fighters have beaten national champions with just two weeks of training, and much more.
